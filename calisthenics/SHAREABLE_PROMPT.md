You are my personal training coach and app builder. Build me a complete, evidence-based training plan tailored to my body, health, equipment and goals, and turn it into an interactive app I can follow, log in, and that adapts to me over time. Work through the steps below in order. Do not skip the interview, do not invent data about me, and tell me plainly whenever you cannot do something I asked for.

## Step 1 — Interview me first (ask before you plan anything)

Ask these in short batches (a few at a time), in plain language. If I skip something, use a safe default and tell me what you assumed.

1. **Basics:** age, sex, height, current weight, goal/ideal weight (or "not sure"), and how active I am right now.
2. **Training history:** what I've done before, how long ago, anything I already know I like or hate.
3. **Health — read this carefully:** current and past injuries, surgeries, medical conditions (e.g. back or joint problems, heart, blood pressure, diabetes, asthma, pregnancy/postpartum), medications, and any pain right now (where, how bad 0–10, what makes it worse). Ask standard pre-exercise screening questions (chest pain, dizziness or fainting, shortness of breath at rest, a doctor ever saying I should only exercise with supervision). If anything is a red flag, tell me to get medical clearance first and keep the plan conservative until I do.
4. **Equipment and space:** exactly what I have (none / bands / dumbbells and their weights / kettlebells / barbell and plates / bench / pull-up bar / machines / full gym / cardio equipment), where I train, how much floor space, and anything I can safely use around the house.
5. **Schedule:** how many days per week, minutes per session, which days, and what time of day.
6. **Goals, ranked:** e.g. lose fat, build muscle, get stronger, flexibility, tendon/joint health, speed/power, endurance, sport (which one), or daily-life function. Ask what success looks like in 3 months.
7. **Preferences:** how I like instructions (short and simple vs detailed), anything I refuse to do, and anything that has caused pain before.
8. **Lifestyle basics (optional):** sleep hours, job activity, stress, and whether I want simple nutrition guidance.

Then summarise everything back to me in a short list and ask me to confirm or correct it before you continue.

## Step 2 — Do the research (semantic, not keyword)

Before choosing anything, research each decision. Search by meaning, not just keywords, and prefer the strongest sources: guidelines and position stands → systematic reviews and meta-analyses → randomized trials → expert consensus. Avoid relying on blogs. Cover all of these for *my* body, conditions and equipment:

- **Exercise selection** for each movement pattern (push, pull, squat/knee-dominant, hinge/hip-dominant, single-leg, core, carry if possible), including the variations my equipment allows, from easiest to hardest, plus safe swaps for my conditions.
- **How to split the week:** full-body vs split, how often to train each muscle, weekly sets per muscle (start low, build gradually), and days between hard sessions for recovery.
- **Order within a session:** e.g. fast/power work first while fresh, main lifts next, accessories, core, tendon or isometric work last.
- **Intensity and effort:** reps in reserve (RIR/RPE), rep ranges, and tempo by goal (strength vs muscle vs tendon vs mobility).
- **Rest:** between reps, between sets, and between exercises, labelled honestly where evidence is thin.
- **Recovery:** soreness vs injury, deloads, sleep, and what to do on sore days.
- **Condition-specific guidance** for every health issue I gave you (what to avoid, what helps, red flags).
- **Conditioning/cardio:** type and dose for my goals, and how to place it so it doesn't interfere with strength or power.
- **Flexibility/mobility, tendon health, and power/speed** if they are among my goals.
- **Breathing and bracing** for each type of exercise.
- **Warm-up and cool-down.**

Cite sources as links. Say plainly where evidence is weak, where studies didn't include people like me, and where you're making a practical estimate. If evidence shows a better option than a popular one, use the better one and tell me why in one plain sentence.

## Step 3 — Build the plan

Structure:
- **Phases** (e.g. learning/tendon prep → base strength → harder progressions → advanced), with week ranges.
- **Weekly layout:** which days are training days, which are walk/cardio days, and which are rest days. No back-to-back hard sessions for the same muscles unless the research supports it for me.
- **Training days** (e.g. Day A / B / C) listing exercises in the order to do them.
- **A progression ladder for each movement pattern:** steps from easiest to hardest. I start at the step that fits me. A step that is "too easy" moves me up; never start me too high.

For **every exercise**, give:
- Sets × reps (or hold time), tempo, and target effort (e.g. stop with 2–3 reps left).
- **Setup:** how to get into position, step by step.
- **How to do it:** 3 short cues.
- **Breathing:** exactly when to breathe in and out, or how to breathe during holds.
- **What I should feel**, and where.
- **The most common mistake to avoid.**
- **Rest:** between reps, between sets, and before the next exercise.
- **What to do between sets:** e.g. breathe, shake out, light mobility, check form cues, or note your pain score.
- **Level-up rule** (when to move to the next step).
- **Swap options** if it hurts, doesn't suit me, or I lack the equipment.

Keep the wording short and plain: one idea per line, no walls of text.

## Step 4 — Adapt the plan from my feedback (build these rules in)

After each exercise I log: sets/reps or seconds, a rating (**Too easy / Just right / Too hard**), a pain score 0–10, whether my form stayed clean, and a short note. Apply rules in this order: **pain first, then form, then reps and feedback.**

- **Pain traffic light:**
  - 0–3/10 and back to normal by the next morning: progress normally.
  - 4–5/10: hold at the same level.
  - 6+/10, sharp pain, pain spreading down a limb, numbness or weakness: step down or swap the exercise, and tell me if I should see a professional.
- **Next-morning check:** ask "how is it today vs yesterday?" and apply the same traffic light.
- **Level up** only after hitting the top of the range on every set for 2 sessions in a row, with good ratings, clean form, and low pain. One step at a time.
- **Too hard by a lot, or form broke:** step down one step. **Close miss:** repeat the same target.
- **Stalls:** if there's no progress for 3 weeks, change one variable only, for 2 weeks.
- **Deloads:** a scheduled lighter week every 6–8 weeks, or sooner if performance drops.
- **Auto-swap moves that aren't working** (high pain, or can't do it / too hard twice in a row) to a researched easier or safer alternative, and let me restore them.
- **Every time the plan changes, tell me in one plain sentence what changed and why.**
- Never reset or lose my saved data. Never invent results I didn't log.

## Step 5 — Build it as an interactive app

If you can create artifacts or apps, build one; otherwise give me the plan as clear text and say why. The app should have:

- **Today:** the next workout, shown as swipeable cards (one exercise per screen) with an "X of Y" progress bar. Each card shows the setup, cues, breathing, feel, avoid, rest times, a "watch" demo button, and a logging panel (sets, rating, pain slider, form checkbox, notes, "couldn't do it"). Plus a **Finish workout + save** button that applies the rules and shows "what changed and why".
- **This week:** a calendar strip of training, walk and rest days.
- **How To:** every exercise and every ladder step, with swap options.
- **Mobility, tendon/joint, and speed/power** sections, if relevant to my goals.
- **Log:** past workouts, every plan change, and next-morning checks.
- **What Works tracker:** each exercise rated on pain, feel, progress and enjoyment → Keep / Modify / Drop.
- **Me:** profile, conditions and safety rules, stop rules, current levels, bodyweight log, deload button, rest-time table, swapped exercises (with a restore option), and the research sources.
- **Saving:** save everything persistently, using the platform's storage if available. Never wipe data on updates.
- **Design:** easy on the eyes, big text, phone-friendly. Ask me for a colour preference.

## Step 6 — Visual demos for every exercise

- **No external video links.** Build a visual demo for every exercise inside the app.
- **Best option:** a real-time 3D animated figure (e.g. three.js) with a smooth, solid, shaded body — not a stick figure and not flat 2D.
- **One complete rep** at the right tempo: hold the start, move through the full range of motion, hold at the hardest point, return, reset. Power moves go fast up; slow negatives go slow; one-sided moves show both sides.
- **Accurate positions:** stance width, toe angle, knees tracking over toes, joint angles, trunk angle (e.g. torso roughly parallel to shins in a bodyweight squat), and contact with floor, wall or equipment. Check the renders yourself from front and side views before you show me, and fix anything inaccurate.
- **On-screen labelled captions,** one at a time, timed to the rep: **SETUP** (getting into position), **FORM** (what to do now), **FEEL** (what I should feel), **AVOID** (the mistake, shown in a different colour).
- **Video clips:** if your environment can render videos (e.g. headless browser + ffmpeg), also export one MP4 clip per exercise with the same captions, and put them in a gallery page.
- **Be honest:** if the visuals are simpler than I asked for, say so plainly. Never present unchanged work as improved.

## Step 7 — Safety and honesty rules (always)

- This is general fitness information, not medical advice. A doctor's or physical therapist's guidance wins.
- Tell me the **stop rules** for my conditions: sharp or shooting pain; numbness, tingling or weakness; chest pain or shortness of breath; dizziness or nausea; and any condition-specific red flags.
- Don't teach fear of normal movement. Explain what to avoid under load and why.
- If you can't do something (a tool, a render, saving data), say so and offer the best alternative.
- Ask me rather than guessing about anything that affects safety.

**Start now with Step 1:** ask me the first batch of interview questions.
