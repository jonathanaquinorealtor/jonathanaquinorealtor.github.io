# Plan review — 2026-11-21

Reviewed the handoff against guidelines, systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Most of the plan holds up. These are the changes and why.

## Changed

1. **Push level corrected to Step 2 = Knee Push-Up (step_index 1).** The saved data and a one-rung move up from wall push-ups both point here. The ladder text marking Negative Push-Up as "Step 2" counted from 0. The app now shows every step counting from 1, the same way as your saved data.
2. **Power goes first in every session**, right after the warm-up. Moves done early are done with less fatigue, so they are faster and stronger. On Nov 20 your Fast Squat was "shakey, hard to balance" after other squats.
   - [NSCA program design (Human Kinetics excerpt)](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/utilize-proper-workout-structure-and-exercise-order)
   - [Exercise-order meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/)
3. **Feeling sick is now a stop rule.** You felt nauseous before the Half-Squat Hold. Nausea during exercise is tied to effort, training fasted, and fluid balance. The fix: a light snack 1–2 h before, sip water, ease in, and stop if it comes back. If it keeps happening, or comes with chest pain or shortness of breath, get checked.
   - [Sports (MDPI) review table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680692/table/sports-07-00162-t001)
4. **Tendon holds progress by getting harder, not longer.** Tendon adaptation depends on load magnitude, not contraction type or length. The best-studied protocol is high strain (~90% max effort) in ~3-second bouts. Bodyweight holds are a practical home version of it.
   - [Bohm 2015 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4532714/)
   - [Schroll 2024](https://doaj.org/article/6de7373a65ab439ea4609085bb4a8f6e)
   - [Arampatzis protocol](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/210/15/2743/17005/Adaptational-responses-of-the-human-Achilles)
   - The 30–45 sec holds come from tendon-pain research (isometrics relieve pain right away). Over weeks they were not better than slow reps. ([Rio 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979840/), [van Ark 2016](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/publications/do-isometric-and-isotonic-exercise-programs-reduce-pain-in-athlet/))
5. **Stretching number corrected.** Holds of 60 sec or more right before training cut performance about 4.6%; holds under 60 sec cut it about 1.1%. The handoff said "4–8%". ([Kay & Blazevich 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659901/), Behm 2016)
6. **Squat clip caption.** The approved clip said "CHEST UP - HIPS BELOW KNEES". That conflicts with your flat-back-depth rule, so it now says "CHEST UP - BACK STAYS FLAT".
7. **Logging additions:** a notes box on every exercise (you write useful notes), a "couldn't do it" option, and "last time" shown on each card.

## Confirmed (no change)

- **90 sec rest.** Resting over 60 sec helps a little; over 90 sec adds nothing measurable for muscle. ([Singer 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/))
- **Big 3 is good but not magic.** Motor-control core work is not clinically better than other exercise for back pain. ([Cochrane, Saragiotto 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26742533/))
- **No lifelong bending ban.** Flexion limits are for early disc symptoms. Exercise is first-line conservative care. ([2024 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2024.1309663/full), [MGH disc protocol](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/MGH/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/rehabilitation-protocol-conservative-management-of-lumbar-spine-disc-displacement.pdf))
- **Volume rises with weekly sets.** Start low and add sets before adding a 4th day. ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28755103/))

# Deep dive — best workouts for your goals (2026-11-21)

I checked 15 more questions against reviews, meta-analyses and the best trials I could find. For each pattern: what the evidence says, and what changed in the plan.

| Goal / pattern | What the evidence says | Plan decision |
|---|---|---|
| Push strength | Push-ups at matched load build strength and muscle about as well as the bench press ([Calatayud 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24983847/), [Kikuchi 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/318024465_Low-load_bench_press_and_push-up_induce_similar_muscle_hypertrophy_and_strength_gain)). Harder variations = more load. | **Keep** the push ladder. |
| Pull strength | The inverted row produced the highest lat and upper-back activity with the lowest low-back load among the rows tested ([Fenwick 2009](https://backfitpro.com/medical-scientific-articles/2009/[80]Fenwick,C.M.J.(2009)Comparison-of-different-rowing-exercises[J.Strength-and-Cond.].pdf)). Floor and suspension versions are similar ([Snarr 2013](https://www.asep.org/asep/asep/JEPonlineDecember2013_Snarr_Esco.pdf)). Bands built strength about as well as weights in the Lopes 2019 meta-analysis (I read it only through summaries); a separate [2025 band meta-analysis in older adults](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12630110/) also found strength gains. | **Changed:** a real pull every pull day. The Doorway Pull Hold now runs alongside the prone ladder on Days B and C. **Strong suggestion:** a $10–20 resistance band with a door anchor would be the single biggest upgrade. |
| Legs | Split squat training matched back squat strength gains in one trial ([Speirs 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8136570)). Deep squats don't add knee injury risk in healthy people ([Hartmann 2013](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Analysis-of-the-Load-on-the-Knee-Joint-and-Column-Hartmann-Wirth/8759c23ad9250bae2038c9980acbd2f1cc995d36)). Depth is limited by your back, not your knees. | **Keep** the legs ladder. Single-leg work matters most at your weight. |
| Glutes / hinge | Single-leg bridges give high glute activity ([EMG summary](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/electromyographic-analysis-of-gluteus-maximus-and-hamstring-activity-during-the-supine-resisted-hip-extension-exercise-versus-supine-unilateral-bridge-to-neutral), [banded single-leg bridge](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10529521/)). Strength training reduces disc-herniation pain ([2026 meta-analysis](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/msc.70231)). | **Changed:** the old Hinge & Core ladder swapped glute work for core holds as you levelled up, so glute loading would have disappeared. It is now a **Hinge & Glute ladder**: bridge → kickstand single-leg bridge → single-leg bridge → slow single-leg bridge. Your saved level (Step 1, Glute Bridge) is unchanged. The no-load wall hinge moved into the warm-up. |
| Core (disc history) | The curl-up gave the best ratio of ab work to spine compression in McGill's lab work. Bird dog and side plank are low-load. Not better than other exercise for back pain overall ([Cochrane](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26742533/)). | **Changed:** the Big 3 is now its own short block every Day A and B, instead of one rung of a ladder. |
| Tendons | Load intensity, not hold time, drives tendon adaptation ([Bohm 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4532714/)). | **Changed:** Day A tendon slot is now **Calf Raise Hold**, since Achilles work was missing. Day A already has two squat-type moves. The **Half-Squat Hold moved to Day C.** |
| Speed / power | Rate of force development improves with heavy strength work and fast-intent (ballistic) work ([RFD review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/269872377_Rate_of_force_development_reliability_improvements_and_influence_on_performance_A_review)). Plyometrics help combat athletes ([meta-analysis](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4663/11/2/33)), but those were trained athletes. | **Keep** the no-jump power ladder first in each session. Jumping stays locked behind the gates. |
| Jumping at 220 lbs | Landing with bent knees cuts knee force. Clinical programs progress by criteria and start at about 60 foot contacts ([MGH plyometric protocol](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/mgh/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/mass-general-jump-and-plyometric-training.pdf)). | **Keep** the gates. The plan's 20–40 contacts is more cautious than clinical programs. Fine at your weight. |
| Fighting fitness | Boxing performance depends on aerobic + anaerobic fitness, upper-body strength and technique ([Chaabene 2015](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Amateur-Boxing:-Physical-and-Physiological-Chaabene-Tabben/433159f838f81302c1dd5e02fac553b7c082f578)). For overweight adults, interval training raises fitness most; walking has the fewest injuries ([network meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40129283/)). | **Added (was missing):** brisk walking 20–30 min, 2–3× a week on off days, with a walk log. Low-impact intervals come in the Months 3–6 stage. |
| Reaction training | Lab reaction gains barely transfer to real sport unless the drill looks like the sport ([combat review](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333000513_The_perceptual_-_Cognitive_skills_of_combat_sports_athletes_A_systematic_review), [team-sport meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39457791/)). | **Keep**, with priority on Called Combos and Hands Up on Cue. Treat the rest as a warm-up. |
| Flexibility | Full-range strength training improves range of motion about as much as stretching ([Alizadeh 2023](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a85d95137b4df232e032d7bd2a0439691605859), [2025 meta-regression](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11841725/)). | **Keep** the 10-min flow. Full range on every rep counts toward flexibility. |
| How much to do | Beginners gain about as much from 1 set as from several at first ([ACSM progression models](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/235653976_Progression_models_in_resistance_training_for_healthy_adults_ACSM_position_stand)). | **Added:** on rough days (sick, shaky, short on time), 1–2 sets still count. Use this before skipping. |

# How the week is split (2026-11-22)

| Question | What the evidence says | Plan decision |
|---|---|---|
| Full body or split? | Split and full-body routines give the same strength and muscle gains when weekly sets are equal ([2024 meta-analysis, 14 studies](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379724552_Efficacy_of_Split_Versus_Full-Body_Resistance_Training_on_Strength_and_Muscle_Growth_A_Systematic_Review_With_Meta-Analysis)). | **Keep full body.** With 3 days, full body hits every muscle 2–3× a week. A split would hit some muscles only once. |
| How often per muscle? | Once weekly sets are matched, training a muscle 1–3× a week makes little difference ([Pelland 2024](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398333408_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_Response_Meta-Regressions_Exploring_the_Effects_of_Weekly_Volume_and_Frequency_on_Muscle_Hypertrophy_and_Strength_Gains_The_Resistance_Training_Dose_ResponseJ_C_Pelland_et)). Twice a week beat once a week in the earlier analysis ([Schoenfeld 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301578131_Effects_of_Resistance_Training_Frequency_on_Measures_of_Muscle_Hypertrophy_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-Analysis)). | **Keep:** push 2× (A, C), pull 2× (B, C), glutes + Big 3 2× (A, B), legs and power 3×. Total weekly sets matter more than which day they land on. |
| Days between sessions? | Untrained people mostly recover in 48 h; a muscle-damage marker stayed high for 48 h and was back to normal by 72 h ([recovery study](https://paulogentil.com/pdf/Time%20course%20of%20recovery%20following%20resistance%20training%20leading%20or%20not%20to%20failure.pdf), [single vs multi-joint recovery](https://digitalcommons.wku.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1653&context=ijes)). | **Keep** a day off between sessions. Max 3 lifting days per 7. |
| Does cardio hurt strength? | Cardio + strength gave the same muscle and strength gains as strength alone (43 studies). The one exception: **explosive strength** gains were blunted ([Schumann 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5407958), [Wilson 2012](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/fulltext/2012/08000/concurrent_training__a_meta_analysis_examining.35.aspx)). | **Walks go on off days, not right before power work.** Brisk walking is low intensity, so interference should be minimal. |

**Your week:** lift every other day (A → B → C rotation), walk 20–30 min on 2–3 off days, take one full rest day. Mobility flow any day. The app's Today tab now shows this week laid out.
