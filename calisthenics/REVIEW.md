# Plan review — 2026-11-21

Reviewed the handoff against guidelines, systematic reviews and meta-analyses. Most of the plan holds up. These are the changes and why.

## Changed

1. **Push level corrected to Step 2 = Knee Push-Up (step_index 1).** The saved data and a one-rung move up from wall push-ups both point here. The ladder text marking Negative Push-Up as "Step 2" counted from 0. The app now shows every step counting from 1, the same way as your saved data.
2. **Power goes first in every session**, right after the warm-up. Moves done early are done with less fatigue, so they are faster and stronger. On Nov 20 your Fast Squat was "shakey, hard to balance" after other squats.
   - [NSCA program design (Human Kinetics excerpt)](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/utilize-proper-workout-structure-and-exercise-order)
   - [Exercise-order meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077380/)
3. **Feeling sick is now a stop rule.** You felt nauseous before the Half-Squat Hold. Nausea during exercise is tied to effort, training fasted, and fluid balance. The fix: a light snack 1–2 h before, sip water, ease in, and stop if it comes back. If it keeps happening, or comes with chest pain or shortness of breath, get checked.
   - [Sports (MDPI) review table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6680692/table/sports-07-00162-t001)
4. **Tendon holds progress by getting harder, not longer.** Tendon adaptation depends on load magnitude, not contraction type or length. The best-studied protocol is high strain (~90% max effort) in ~3-second bouts. Bodyweight holds are a practical home version of it.
   - [Bohm 2015 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4532714/)
   - [Schroll 2024](https://doaj.org/article/6de7373a65ab439ea4609085bb4a8f6e)
   - [Arampatzis protocol](https://cob.silverchair.com/jeb/article/210/15/2743/17005/Adaptational-responses-of-the-human-Achilles)
   - The 30–45 sec holds come from tendon-pain research (isometrics relieve pain right away). Over weeks they were not better than slow reps. ([Rio 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25979840/), [van Ark 2016](https://researchprofiles.canberra.edu.au/en/publications/do-isometric-and-isotonic-exercise-programs-reduce-pain-in-athlet/))
5. **Stretching number corrected.** Holds of 60 sec or more right before training cut performance about 4.6%; holds under 60 sec cut it about 1.1%. The handoff said "4–8%". ([Kay & Blazevich 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21659901/), Behm 2016)
6. **Squat clip caption.** The approved clip said "CHEST UP - HIPS BELOW KNEES". That conflicts with your flat-back-depth rule, so it now says "CHEST UP - BACK STAYS FLAT".
7. **Logging additions:** a notes box on every exercise (you write useful notes), a "couldn't do it" option, and "last time" shown on each card.

## Confirmed (no change)

- **90 sec rest.** Resting over 60 sec helps a little; over 90 sec adds nothing measurable for muscle. ([Singer 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/))
- **Big 3 is good but not magic.** Motor-control core work is not clinically better than other exercise for back pain. ([Cochrane, Saragiotto 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26742533/))
- **No lifelong bending ban.** Flexion limits are for early disc symptoms. Exercise is first-line conservative care. ([2024 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2024.1309663/full), [MGH disc protocol](https://www.massgeneral.org/assets/MGH/pdf/orthopaedics/sports-medicine/physical-therapy/rehabilitation-protocol-conservative-management-of-lumbar-spine-disc-displacement.pdf))
- **Volume rises with weekly sets.** Start low and add sets before adding a 4th day. ([Schoenfeld 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28755103/))
